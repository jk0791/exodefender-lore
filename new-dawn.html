The Fall of New Dawn

An account compiled from the colony's final transmissions and the historical archives of the Interstellar Settlement Initiative.

The Founding of New Dawn

In 2186, Mara Venn became the first human to travel beyond the Solar System, arriving on Terra-7h through a quantum matter-transfer receiver assembled years earlier.

The settlement that followed, named New Dawn, became humanity's first extraterrestrial colony.

Situated in a temperate valley on Terra-7h's largest continent, New Dawn occupied a landscape remarkably similar to the temperate regions of Earth. Within its first year, 71 people had made the one-way journey to their new home.

In 2188, the first human child was born beyond Earth.

By 2196, New Dawn's population had reached 427. The settlement was thriving, and interest in emigration was growing rapidly. More than 75,000 people were on the waiting list for passage from Earth.

For the first time in human history, the prospect of establishing permanent communities among the stars seemed not merely possible, but inevitable.

First Contact

On [date], 2208, twenty-two years after the first landing, New Dawn made contact with an extraterrestrial intelligence.

Although robotic reconnaissance missions had discovered microbial life on numerous worlds, no evidence of multicellular organisms had ever been found beyond Earth, much less intelligent life.

The discovery was unprecedented.

At the time, neither the colonists nor the authorities on Earth knew anything about the civilisation responsible for the contact.

Its name, its origins and its intentions were entirely unknown.

Humanity would eventually come to know it as the C'tarr-ni.

Over the following days, rudimentary communication was established between New Dawn and the extraterrestrial visitors. Despite the enormous linguistic and cultural barriers, both civilisations appeared eager to learn about one another.

The exchange was initially regarded as an extraordinary success. For the first time in human history, humanity was communicating with another intelligent species.

Then the visitors learned that Terra-7h was not humanity's homeworld.

Their response was immediate.

A final communication was received from the visitors.

Its meaning would be debated for years. Eventually, however, a consensus emerged among humanity's linguists: the communication expressed a single concept.

Heresy.

All communication ceased.

For three days, repeated attempts by New Dawn and Earth to re-establish contact went unanswered.

The next contact would be the first physical encounter between the two species.

The Massacre

On [date], 2208, three C'tarr-ni attack craft descended from the skies above New Dawn.

Without warning or any attempt at communication, they opened fire on the settlement.

Within twenty minutes, New Dawn had been devastated. Its habitats, infrastructure and most of its population were destroyed. The attacking craft departed as abruptly as they had arrived.

A handful of colonists survived the initial assault, sheltering among the remains of the settlement.

For seven days, they maintained intermittent communication with Earth.

They knew that rescue was impossible. The quantum transit system could not deliver the equipment and personnel necessary to save them.

Nevertheless, the survivors continued transmitting eyewitness accounts, recordings and technical observations of the attack.

Their reports provided humanity with its first detailed intelligence concerning C'tarr-ni weapons, aircraft and military capabilities. Much of what would later become humanity's understanding of its enemy began with those final transmissions.

On [date], the last communication from New Dawn was received.

No further transmissions followed.

Despite repeated attempts to re-establish contact with the C'tarr-ni, no response was ever received.

The following year, humanity attempted to establish two smaller colonies on distant worlds. Both were discovered and destroyed.

The attacks followed the same pattern: no warning, overwhelming force and the complete destruction of the settlements.

It was now clear that New Dawn had not been an isolated tragedy.

Humanity's expansion into the stars had brought it into conflict with a civilisation that would not tolerate its presence.
